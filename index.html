Why we build for nuclear

Nuclear power runs around the clock and emits about as much carbon over its life as wind. It has one of the lowest death rates per unit of electricity of any source. Every figure below comes from a peer-reviewed or intergovernmental source, cited under the chart.

Between 1971 and 2009, by replacing fossil-fuel power, nuclear plants worldwide avoided

64 billiontonnes of CO₂-equivalent emissions

1.84 milliondeaths from air pollution

Kharecha, P.A. & Hansen, J.E. (2013). Prevented mortality and greenhouse gas emissions from historical and projected nuclear power. Environmental Science & Technology 47(9), 4889–4895. doi:10.1021/es3051197

Those plumes are water vapour

A cooling tower releases warm water vapour from the plant's cooling circuit. Nothing is burned to raise a nuclear plant's steam, so there is no combustion smoke.

India's nuclear build-out has barely begun

In 2024–25 India's reactors generated a record 56,681 million units, about 3.1% of the country's electricity, and avoided around 49 million tonnes of CO₂. The target for 2047 is at least 100 GW, more than eleven times today's capacity. Each new unit starts with excavation, foundation rafts, containment structures and kilometres of fabricated piping: the work OECC does.
